How trades searches actually work

When a Birmingham homeowner searches "emergency plumber near me" at 9pm, they are not comparing websites or reading reviews carefully. They're looking at the first three results on Google Maps and calling the one that looks most established. The decision takes about fifteen seconds.

That's the mechanic. It applies to almost every trades search — plumbers, electricians, roofers, heating engineers, locksmiths. The searches are short, the intent is urgent, and the person who ranks first gets the call. The business ranked fourth gets nothing, even if they're better, cheaper, and closer.

The problem with Checkatrade and MyBuilder

We're not against Checkatrade — plenty of Birmingham tradespeople use it and get work from it. The issue is dependency. Every lead you get from Checkatrade comes through their platform, at their price, on their terms. They can increase their fees, change how profiles are ranked, or decide tomorrow that they're expanding into your service category. You have no control over any of that.

The specific thing most Birmingham tradespeople don't realise: when someone searches "plumber Birmingham" on Google, the results they see are Google Maps listings — not Checkatrade profiles. Checkatrade appears as a website result below the map pack, which most people don't scroll to once they've seen the map. Your Google Business Profile is the asset that captures those top-of-page positions. Checkatrade doesn't give you one of those.

Local SEO builds something you own. When we optimise your Google Business Profile and build your local citation presence, those assets belong to you outright. They keep working whether or not you're paying anyone. The calls they generate cost you nothing per enquiry. That's a fundamentally different economics from any lead generation platform.

Reviews — the thing that actually moves your ranking

Of all the signals Google uses to rank trades businesses in the local pack, reviews are the most visible, the most controllable, and the most neglected. A Birmingham plumber with 80 five-star reviews and a 4.8 rating is essentially in a different category to one with 12 reviews and a 4.3 — they rank differently, they convert differently, and they attract different quality work.

The problem isn't that tradespeople don't do good work. It's that nobody ever asks for the review at the right moment. We build a simple system — a text message sent after each job with a direct link to your Google review page — that makes asking effortless and consistent.

The businesses at the top of Google Maps for "plumber Birmingham", "electrician Birmingham", and similar searches typically have 60–200+ reviews. The businesses that currently rank there got there primarily by accumulating reviews consistently over 12–24 months — not through any technical trick. We build the foundation and the system; the reviews accumulate as you work.
